Frontier ocean technology

Regulatory uncertainty is a design input, not a legal afterthought.

Wave-powered compute, autonomous fleets, ocean carbon removal and seabed mining are moving offshore faster than their rules. Projects stall when an agency asks for science nobody collected. Collect it in year one.

The five questions I start with Read: Data centers on the high seas

What a regulator will want in year three

  1. Baselines. What the site looked like before you arrived.
  2. Operating effects. Noise, light, collision and entanglement.
  3. Cumulative effects. The whole fleet, not one unit.
  4. Monitoring. A plan the agency can check.
  5. End of life. How lost and retired units come back.

Background

I learned the ocean as a scientist and a forecaster before I learned its law.

As a legal intern in the International Section of NOAA's Office of General Counsel, I drafted language on the legal status of sea ice for U.S. interagency negotiations on the IMO Polar Code. My research has since contributed to International Seabed Authority deliberations on the Clarion-Clipperton Zone, and my cumulative-impacts research informed the High Seas Treaty (BBNJ) negotiations.
