Frontier ocean technology

The ocean is becoming infrastructure. Its rules are still in draft.

Ocean ventures are heading into water the rules haven't reached. I help them map the regulatory terrain first, alongside their counsel. Early movers shape the terms.

Polymetallic nodules on the abyssal plain. NOAA Ocean Exploration, 2026 Cook Islands ROV Exploration.

Four things I'd tell any founder heading offshore

"No rule applies" is not a strategy. A technology that fits no category gets classified by whoever moves first, usually a regulator. In 2024, Bay Area regulators publicly warned that an unpermitted underwater data-center test in San Francisco Bay could draw fines.

Collect the science early. Baselines can't be gathered after the fact, and they are what an agency or court will weigh.

The texts are still open. The first Conference of the Parties to the High Seas Treaty (BBNJ) meets in January 2027. The International Maritime Organization's new code for autonomous ships is voluntary and covers cargo ships only. The International Seabed Authority (ISA) has no final Mining Code.

Diligence is coming either way. Investors and insurers will ask how the rules treat your platform and who pays when one is lost. A clear answer is a fundraising asset.

Where the frontier is

Technologies outrunning their rulebooks

Ocean-based compute

Wave-powered nodes running AI at sea. Panthalassa's $140 million Series B, announced in May 2026, made it a funded category.

Wave & tidal energy

Moving from test sites to arrays. The hurdles: BOEM leasing and FERC licensing, acoustics, entanglement, monitoring.

Ocean carbon removal

Alkalinity enhancement and biomass sinking now face the London Protocol's new assessment frameworks and buyers' verification science.

Autonomous vessels & platforms

Uncrewed craft under rules written for crews: flag, lights, collision avoidance.

Seabed minerals

An unfinished ISA Mining Code, a parallel U.S. track and thin deep-sea science.

Ocean data & sensing

Coastal-state consent for research in foreign waters, data rights and dual use.

The method

Five questions to settle, with your counsel, before someone else asks them

01

What is it under the rules?

Ship, installation or neither. The UN Convention on the Law of the Sea never defines "ship."

02

Whose rules apply?

Each ocean zone assigns rights differently. So does the flag you fly, or don't.

03

What review is triggered?

NEPA and coastal-zone review at home. BBNJ Part IV assessment beyond national jurisdiction.

04

What science will regulators demand?

Which studies each review expects, and how far ahead each must start.

05

What happens at end of life?

Recovery duties and dumping rules shape design and cost.
