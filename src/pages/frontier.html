Frontier ocean technology

The ocean is becoming infrastructure.

Compute, energy, carbon removal, autonomy, minerals: a new generation of ocean ventures is moving from prototype to deployment. Most of them are entering legal territory nobody has mapped yet. Sarah helps them map it before regulators, investors or challengers do.

Where the frontier is

Technologies outrunning their rulebooks

Ocean-based computing

Autonomous, wave-powered platforms that run AI on board and send results ashore by satellite. Panthalassa's 2026 Series B made this a funded industry. The open questions: legal status, jurisdiction over data and hardware, and review on the high seas.

Wave & tidal energy

Marine energy is moving from test sites to arrays. The hard parts are licensing pathways, acoustic and entanglement science, and the monitoring regulators will require.

Ocean carbon removal

Alkalinity enhancement, seaweed sinking and other marine CDR approaches have to work within dumping rules, research permits and the verification science that carbon markets rely on.

Autonomous vessels & platforms

Uncrewed surface and subsea systems sit uneasily in rules written for crewed ships. The IMO's work on autonomous ships is changing that.

Seabed resources

Deep-sea minerals pit commercial timelines against unfinished regulations and scientific uncertainty about deep-ocean ecosystems.

Ocean data & sensing

Persistent ocean observation raises questions of marine scientific research consent, data rights and dual use.

The method

Five questions, answered before someone else asks them

01

What is it in law?

Ship, installation, device or something new. The answer decides which regimes apply, and the law of the sea doesn't define "ship."

02

Whose law applies?

Territorial sea, exclusive economic zone, continental shelf and high seas each assign rights differently. So does flag-state registration.

03

What review is triggered?

Domestic environmental review where deployed, and impact assessment duties for activities beyond national jurisdiction under the High Seas Treaty.

04

What science will regulators demand?

Baselines, acoustic and entanglement risk, cumulative effects, and the monitoring that will show it all holds up.

05

What happens at end of life?

Recovery obligations, marine debris and dumping rules shape both design and cost.
