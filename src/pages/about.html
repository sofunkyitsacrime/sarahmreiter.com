About

Sarah M. Reiter

Ocean policy and regulatory strategist. Naval Academy oceanographer. J.D. in ocean and coastal law. Co-editor of the forthcoming third edition of the American Bar Association treatise Ocean and Coastal Law and Policy.

Sarah M. Reiter started with the science. She studied oceanography at the U.S. Naval Academy, was commissioned into the U.S. Air Force, served as a weather officer, supported operations after Hurricanes Katrina and Rita, and later led a forecasting team serving 82 military installations.

Then she turned to the rules: a J.D. cum laude in ocean and coastal law at Vermont Law School. As a legal intern at NOAA's Office of General Counsel, she drafted language on the legal status of sea ice for U.S. interagency negotiations on the IMO Polar Code. At Stanford's Center for Ocean Solutions she co-authored "Managing mining of the deep seabed" (Science, 2015).

She has since lobbied on federal bills at Monterey Bay Aquarium, co-owned Upwelling Consulting, LLC (2019–2022), taught ocean and coastal law at Vermont Law and Graduate School, served as co-PI on Oxford Seascape Ecology Lab grants, and led delegations to the UN climate negotiations, the UN Ocean Conference and the IUCN Congress. She leads ocean solutions work at the New England Aquarium. Her research has contributed to International Seabed Authority deliberations and informed the High Seas Treaty negotiations.
