About

Sarah M. Reiter

Ocean policy and regulatory strategist. Naval Academy oceanographer. J.D. in ocean and coastal law. Co-editor of the forthcoming third edition of the American Bar Association treatise Ocean and Coastal Law and Policy.

[SARAH] headshot

Sarah M. Reiter started with the science. She studied oceanography at the U.S. Naval Academy and wrote her honors thesis on Antarctic polynyas. Commissioned into the U.S. Air Force from the Academy, she served as a weather officer, supported operations after Hurricanes Katrina and Rita, and later led a 40-person team forecasting for 82 military installations.

Then she turned to the rules. Reiter earned a J.D. cum laude in ocean and coastal law at Vermont Law School. As a legal intern in the International Section of NOAA's Office of General Counsel, she drafted language on the legal status of sea ice for U.S. interagency negotiations on the IMO Polar Code. As a fellow at Stanford's Center for Ocean Solutions (2013–15), she co-authored "Managing mining of the deep seabed" (Science, 2015) and a study of cumulative impacts in U.S. environmental review.

At Monterey Bay Aquarium she lobbied on federal bills, including the Microbead-Free Waters Act. She co-owned Upwelling Consulting, LLC (2019–2022) and teaches ocean and coastal law at Vermont Law and Graduate School. She has served as co-PI on three grants with the Oxford Seascape Ecology Lab, and leads ocean solutions work at the New England Aquarium. She has led delegations to the UN climate negotiations, the UN Ocean Conference and the IUCN World Conservation Congress.

Her research has contributed to International Seabed Authority deliberations on the Clarion-Clipperton Zone, and her cumulative-impacts research informed the High Seas Treaty (BBNJ) negotiations. Her advisory practice brings that range to new ocean technology, working alongside clients' counsel.
