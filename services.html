Advisory services

Organized around the problem in front of you.

Each engagement starts from one question: what decision do you need to make, and what science and law stand between you and making it well?

01

Frontier technology regulatory strategy

For ocean-tech founders, investors and their counsel

New ocean technologies often fit no existing category. A floating compute node, an autonomous sensor fleet, an ocean-alkalinity pilot: each raises questions about legal status, jurisdiction and review that no agency has answered yet. Getting those answers early shapes design, siting and fundraising.

What you get

  • A regulatory risk map: legal characterization, jurisdiction by deployment zone, triggered reviews, open questions ranked by impact
  • Permitting pathway and timeline for pilots and commercial scale-up
  • Investor- and board-ready regulatory memo for diligence
  • Early engagement strategy with regulators and stakeholders
02

Permitting & environmental review strategy

For offshore energy developers and project teams

Permitting delays are rarely about paperwork. They come from science gaps the reviewing agency will eventually ask about. Sarah identifies those gaps up front and makes sure the baseline studies, monitoring plans and impact analyses answer the questions regulators and challengers will ask.

What you get

  • Scientific and legal readiness review before filing
  • EIS/EA strategy and independent review of draft analyses
  • Marine mammal, acoustic and protected-species compliance strategy
  • Comment and response strategy during review
03

International ocean governance

For companies, NGOs and governments operating beyond national waters

The High Seas Treaty (the BBNJ Agreement) is now in force, the IMO is building rules for autonomous ships, and seabed-mining rules remain contested. What happens in those negotiations will decide whether, and how, activities beyond national jurisdiction can proceed.

What you get

  • Treaty and negotiation briefings tailored to your activities
  • Environmental impact assessment strategy for areas beyond national jurisdiction
  • Flag-state and jurisdiction analysis for vessels and platforms
  • Positioning and submissions for international processes
04

Science–law translation for counsel

For maritime and environmental law firms, general counsel

Ocean cases turn on science that most legal teams cannot evaluate in-house. Sarah reads the record the way a scientist would and explains it the way a court will need to hear it.

What you get

  • Scientific review of briefs, administrative records and expert reports
  • Identification and vetting of expert witnesses
  • Issue framing for litigation and agency challenges
  • Cumulative-impacts analysis in environmental review records
05

Policy strategy for funders & agencies

For foundations, ocean funds, agencies and coastal communities

Good ocean policy is measurable, enforceable and grounded in what the science can actually show. Sarah helps funders and public bodies decide where to invest effort and how to design rules and programs that last.

What you get

  • Governance landscape and opportunity analysis
  • Grant and portfolio strategy for ocean policy funds
  • Policy design, stakeholder process and implementation support
