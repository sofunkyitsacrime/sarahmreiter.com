About

Sarah M. Reiter

Oceanographer. Attorney. Published researcher. Twenty years of turning ocean data into decisions that governments, industry and communities act on.

[SARAH] headshot

Sarah came to ocean law through the ocean itself. As an oceanography student at the U.S. Naval Academy, she used satellite imagery to study the formation of Antarctic polynyas. She was commissioned into the U.S. Air Force, where she led a 40-person operational weather team forecasting for 82 military installations, including support in the aftermath of Hurricanes Katrina and Rita.

She then trained in law at Vermont Law School (J.D., cum laude, Ocean & Coastal Law). In the International Section of NOAA's Office of General Counsel, she drafted language on the legal status of sea ice for U.S. negotiations on the IMO Polar Code, and prepared attorneys for fisheries and law-of-the-sea negotiations and hearings. As an Early Career Law and Policy Fellow at Stanford's Center for Ocean Solutions, she co-authored work on deep-seabed mining in Science and on cumulative impacts in environmental review in the Stanford Environmental Law Journal.

Since then she has worked across the full range of ocean uses: fishing and aquaculture, shipping, deep-seabed mining and offshore renewables. Her research informed negotiations of the BBNJ Agreement and contributed to International Seabed Authority deliberations on protected areas in the Clarion-Clipperton Zone. She facilitated work on future shipping measures at the biodiversity–climate nexus with the IMO and World Maritime University. She has led delegations to the UN climate negotiations, the UN Ocean Conference, the IUCN World Conservation Congress and the Our Ocean Conference.

She previously co-owned Upwelling Consulting, LLC (2019–2022), advising on climate adaptation legislation, endangered species recovery strategy and comparative international law. She has taught Ocean and Coastal Law and Environmental Dispute Resolution at Vermont Law and Graduate School, collaborates with the Oxford Seascape Ecology Lab as co-PI on grants linking geospatial science to policy, and leads ocean solutions work at the New England Aquarium.
